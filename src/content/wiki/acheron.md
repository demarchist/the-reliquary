Acheron is the Plane of Endless War: an infinite grey void crowded with tumbling metal cubes that range from the size of dice to the size of planetoids. Every face may hold its own battlefield, settlement, or marching host, and gravity belongs locally to each face. Crossing an edge means passing from one field of gravity into another; crossing between moving cubes may require a leap over the void with no promise of landing.

## An Economy of Strength

The first society the party encountered was a smoky feasting hall of Goblins, Orcs, and Hobgoblins built from scrap metal. Its hierarchy was immediate and brutally legible. The newcomers were attacked; [[Fëanon Sërindë|Fëanon]] put most of the charge to sleep; the party killed the largest Orc and his entourage; the hall acclaimed them as its new apex.

[[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]] then did what Acheron rewards. He turned victory into a story and the story into an army, promising a High Elf to hunt, a book to seize, and battles along the way. Their following absorbed a defeated rival host until it numbered in the hundreds. Its loyalty appeared rooted less in friendship or ideology than in demonstrated power, continually renewed through momentum and conquest.

That makes Acheron comprehensible without making it safe. The party did not conquer the plane; it learned enough of the plane's grammar to move through it.

## Death After Death

The goblinoids of Acheron are already dead. When rival warbosses were slain, their bodies disintegrated as unseen machinations reclaimed and recycled their souls. That process did not make individual destruction meaningless—the [[Warform]] still slaughtered the soldiers gathered around it—but it did make battle inexhaustible. Defeat feeds the system that produces the next war.

The limits of that bondage became visible when the party opened a Gate to [[Thunder Bluff]]. Goblinoid petitioners carried pieces of the [[The Acheron Spelljammer|Acheron wreck]] through the portal, took only a few steps on the Material Plane, and lost corporeality. Their exact disposition was not observed. The wood and metal remained, showing that Acheron's hold over its petitioners does not extend to every piece of matter they carry.

## The Larger War

[[Dean Rochester]] described Acheron as one of the planes across which the Blood War of the Tanar'ri plays out. The party did not encounter that wider fiendish conflict directly, but the detail places the goblinoid hosts within something larger than local raids: Acheron is both an afterlife organised around recurring battle and terrain used by wars that cross the planes.

## The Warform

The most complete expression of the plane the party encountered was the [[Warform]], a silent, oily amalgam of hundreds of warriors and beasts. Goblinoids knelt at the sight of it and cried out its name. It landed among those same worshippers and slaughtered them indiscriminately.

Reverence therefore offered no protection. The Warform behaved less like the champion of a faction than war given a body—an aggregation of the souls Acheron continually breaks down and returns to conflict. That interpretation fits what the party saw, but the creature's origin and place in the plane's machinery remain unknown.

Fëanon did not try to overpower it. He waited until it crossed between cube faces and used Wish to reverse its gravity, sending it falling upward into the void. The Warform vanished; it was not confirmed destroyed.

## Modron Roads

[[Modrons]] march across Acheron's cubes, and an injured Modron told [[Gundren Rockseeker|Gundren]] of a Modron channel or passage ahead, in the same direction as Candlekeep's missing book. The party never reached or identified that channel. Its existence suggests that the apparent chaos of Acheron's battlefields is crossed by ordered routes understood by the beings of Mechanus—perhaps one infrastructure laid through another philosophy's afterlife.

The repaired Modron's fate is likewise unknown. It joined the march toward the wreck, but the account does not establish whether it reached the passage, remained with the abandoned war party, or found another way home.

## The Acheron Expedition

The party came to Acheron at [[Candlekeep]]'s request, carrying an overdue borrower's Ioun stone and a loaned planar fork that Candlekeep required them to replace with one procured on Acheron. No such acquisition is recorded; the party later sent Candlekeep a planar fork, but whether it was the loan or an unrecorded replacement remains unresolved. The stone led Fëanon to a ruined spelljammer haunted by sword-wraiths and an [[Allips|Allip]] believed—but never confirmed—to be the High Elf borrower. The group crossed multiple faces of the Great Cube, destroyed the Allip, recovered the century-overdue book from the captain's quarters, and salvaged most of the stripped wreck into the Barony.

They returned the book and a planar fork to Candlekeep and received the Mandolin sought by JmJm. The expedition succeeded. Acheron continued exactly as it had before them.
