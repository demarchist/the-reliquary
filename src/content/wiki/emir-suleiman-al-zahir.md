Emir Suleiman al-Zahir is the custodian of the Elminster Annex at [[Candlekeep]]. When the party Teleported directly into the collection, its arcane armour and painted guardians came to life and a gale gathered into al-Zahir's storm-edged humanoid form. He did not attack immediately. He identified the intrusion, established what the visitors wanted, refused to surrender the protected Mandolin, and escorted them to the authority who could negotiate.

That response defines his role better than the word *guard*. Al-Zahir is the individual judgment within the Annex's formidable security system: he had force available, but his first response was stewardship rather than violence.

## Ossa and the Borrowed Shape

[[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]] asked why al-Zahir allowed himself to be enslaved at Candlekeep and claimed unusual insight into Djinn because he had previously used True Polymorph to become one. Al-Zahir rejected the claim as cultural appropriation. A mortal who borrows a body does not thereby acquire the history of a people, the customs of its houses, or the experience of living in the grand city of Ossa.

The rebuke was not merely annoyance at Cirrus. It was a statement of personhood. Al-Zahir refuses to let the powers of his species stand in for its culture, or a visitor's spell stand in for his life.

Cirrus's question should not be taken as evidence that al-Zahir is enslaved. The Djinni never confirmed the premise, and the exact compact by which he serves Candlekeep is unknown. His title of Emir, invocation of Ossa's noble houses, independent discretion inside the Annex, and ability to assume a more human guise all point to an identity larger than his post.

## Between Security and Scholarship

Al-Zahir serves as the working bridge between [[Dean Rochester]]'s policy and the Annex's defenses. He opened the Dean's office and visited the party's cottage the following morning to deliver Candlekeep's rusted planar fork immediately before their journey to [[Acheron]]. The separately negotiated Abyssal fork did not arrive until after the expedition. When the party later returned Candlekeep's book and a planar fork, the institution honoured its bargain and released the Mandolin; whether the returned fork was the loan or a replacement procured on Acheron is not recorded.

Nothing in the exchange suggests al-Zahir was personally swayed by [[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]]'s gold or theatrics. He contained an intrusion, conveyed the visitors to the person empowered to make exceptions, and executed the resulting agreement. In a session full of personalities trying to redefine the rules around themselves, al-Zahir simply did his job without surrendering himself to it.
