Ceregorach, the Laughing God, is the patron of [[The Warlocks of Ceregorach|a scattered cadre of warlocks]] bound together by teeth, suffering, and the claim that cruelty is a path to strength. The children's name is the Tooth Fairy. The form [[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]] knew personally was [[Mediocris Dente]]: a gaunt, winged Archfey draped in necklaces of teeth who exacted nightly tribute and held his servant conscious in an empty dream-world when displeased.

Older descriptions of Ceregorach as a benevolent trickster do not fit the campaign record. His servants terrorise victims, mutilate bodies, build simulacra from teeth, defend suffering as spiritually useful, and turn the memory of abuse into a weapon. If the patron believes these acts serve a greater joke or purpose, no victim has been shown the benevolence.

## The Theology of Suffering

[[Bilrigger]] gave the clearest account of the cult's internal logic. Everything Ceregorach did to Cirrus, he argued, made the warlock stronger. Pain was cultivation; survival proved the value of the one who inflicted it. Selûne's loving gifts were supposedly another leash because she could withdraw them, while Ceregorach's cruelty made no dishonest claim to kindness.

This is not chaos without a philosophy. It is a philosophy that converts the victim's resilience into praise for the abuser. Cirrus rejected it because whatever strength he gained was his own, and because pain that happens to produce growth does not become a gift retroactively.

## Teeth

Teeth are tribute, badge, body, and threat within Ceregorach's network.

Cirrus surrendered one beneath his pillow every night. [[Strigu]] tore the teeth and lips from forty-three victims and carried a jade tooth marking his pact. The Bilrigger who debated Cirrus proved to be a simulacrum made from thousands of teeth, while the real Derro's green molar was split with his skull. In the Bloodstone Lands, [[Zulkhir Atlan Issan]] sent thousands of bloody teeth to the [[Fane of Selune]] and heaped them around the feet of Cirrus's own statue.

The symbol works because a tooth is intimate and replaceable but not freely expendable. One tribute seems small. Repetition turns a body into an account from which the patron makes withdrawals. A pile of thousands turns individual surrender into the visible scale of the network's harm.

## A Death That Did Not Hold

Cirrus and [[Horatio Von Bumidere|Buumi]] killed Mediocris Dente in the collapsing Bog, and Cirrus crushed the creature's head with a giant amethyst. The pact broke. Later he burned its remains and the teeth he carried.

Yet Strigu soon insisted Ceregorach was not dead. Warlocks continued to receive power. A moving portrait in Bilrigger's vault showed the same vast-mouthed figure Cirrus recognised as his old master. Ceregorach spoke through a tooth-filled pit, and Atlan still serves the Tooth Fairy years after Dente's destruction.

Several explanations remain possible. Dente may have been an avatar; an Archfey may reform after bodily death; the name Ceregorach may belong to a greater being that acted through Dente; or the warlocks may collectively sustain something their former master left behind. What is certain is that Cirrus killed the body that held his pact and gained real freedom. What he killed was not the whole threat.

## The Last Two

Selûne originally directed Cirrus toward four of Ceregorach's warlocks: a halfling in Neverwinter, a Derro beneath Gracklstugh, a Red Wizard, and a samurai in Kara-Tur. Strigu and Bilrigger are dead. Atlan is the Red Wizard. The surviving samurai is very likely [[Samonosuke]], who sent [[Letji]] to spy on Cirrus.

Atlan now calls himself one of only two servants of the Tooth Fairy who remain. The count matches Cirrus's original quarry, though it need not encompass every lesser pact or hidden cultist. The network has narrowed enough to become personal: two active servants, a former warlock who refuses the patron's claim, and a patron whose apparent bodily death failed to settle whether it can die at all.
