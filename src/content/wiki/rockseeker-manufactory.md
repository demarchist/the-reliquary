The Rockseeker Manufactory is a massive, sprawling industrial complex on the rim of the Rauvin's gorge, just outside the western wall of [[selunia|New Daggerford]] and beside [[thunder-rapids|Hammerfall]]. A millrace taken off the river above the falls drives its hammers and wheels. Built by Gundren Rockseeker, it is a hub of industry and innovation where skilled craftsmen and engineers create some of the finest weapons and armor in all of Faerûn.

## Operations

The Manufactory contains forges, foundries, and workshops filled with the sounds of hammering, grinding, and the roar of flames. The air is thick with black smoke and the grounds are coated with coal ash. Wagons loaded with materials are constantly coming and going. Despite the noise and chaos, the complex is well-organized and efficient.

Gundren built and owns the Manufactory, but day-to-day operations are run by his brothers Barthen and Nundro, with Smith Slagbelcher — relocated from Sundabar at Gundren's request — serving as foreman. The Rockseekers are constantly experimenting with new techniques and materials.

## Economic Impact

The Manufactory is an important part of the economy of Thunder Bluff and a major employer in the region. The Rockseekers are respected members of the community, though they are not known for their generosity nor their willingness to help others.

## Planar Salvage

The Manufactory has become the Barony's clearinghouse for technology recovered beyond Faerûn. Its workers began cataloguing the dismantled remains of [[The Acheron Spelljammer|the Acheron spelljammer]] while Barthen and Nundro organised an expedition to excavate [[The Derelict|the Modron vessel]] beneath the River Surbrin.

The projects look similar only from a distance. One is a stripped, possibly Lunar-Elven cultural artifact recovered from a battlefield; the other is a confirmed Modron wreck whose core killed [[Cornubel]] and embedded fragments of alien minds in the Rockseeker family. Treating both as ordinary salvage would be efficient and dangerously incomplete. The Manufactory now sits at the boundary between workshop and archaeological institution, without any clear evidence that its procedures have caught up to the change.
