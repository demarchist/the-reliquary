Letji was a bowyer who arrived in [[Thunder Bluff]] while the party was in [[Al Askar]]. He was later exposed as a spy sent by [[Samonosuke]], a [[The Warlocks of Ceregorach|Warlock of Ceregorach]], to watch [[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]].

Letji is not known to have been a warlock or pact-holder himself. His connection to the faction ran through Samonosuke, whom he named as his master.

## Capture and Death

After [[Crisserei]] granted Cirrus awareness of hostile creatures within the Barony, Cirrus remembered the recently arrived bowyer and ordered his arrest. Letji attempted to flee. Once caught, he spat at Cirrus and declared that the Warlocks of Ceregorach would all die. He then bit down on a concealed object, causing his head to explode.

The statement is difficult to reconcile neatly with his service to Samonosuke. It could have been a threat that treated the former warlock Cirrus as one of the doomed, a warning about the faction, an expression of hostility among Ceregorach's servants, or something else entirely. Letji died before anyone could clarify it.

His body was taken to the [[Fane of Selune]] for later questioning.

## The Soul That Refused

The party attempted to restore Letji through reincarnation. His remains dissolved into the ritual oils, but no new body formed because his soul did not wish to return.

Cirrus and [[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]] then called upon divine intervention to petition the spirit with five questions. Letji disclosed his name, identified Samonosuke as his master, and revealed the spying mission. Cirrus assumed that such an agent possessed a dark soul and offered it to Crisserei so that it might be saved.

Crisserei corrected him. Selûne had not compelled Letji to answer; when petitioned by the goddess, he had chosen to cooperate. His was the soul of a good person rather than an evildoer. Shaken by the discovery, Cirrus declined to ask the final question and ended the spell.

These are three separate facts: Letji refused reincarnation, voluntarily answered Selûne, and was judged good. None proves that he volunteered for Samonosuke's mission. Conversely, being good does not prove that he was coerced or innocent of wrongdoing.

## An Unresolved Allegiance

Letji's choices resist a simple account of fanatic loyalty. Destroying himself under capture could reflect devotion, fear, coercion, or a determination to protect information. Refusing life again could reflect peace with death, fear of returning, or commitment to a purpose that had already ended. The record does not distinguish among them.

His case therefore complicates what is known of Ceregorach's surviving network. Samonosuke can command the service of someone whom Selûne recognised as good, but it remains unknown whether he inspires loyalty, applies coercion, or pursues a cause that Letji considered morally defensible. Letji's testimony exposes the limits of treating every person around the Tooth Fairy's warlocks as merely another extension of the patron.
