## The Elminster Annex

The alarm became a gale.

Arcane armour stepped down from plinths throughout the display hall. Painted figures turned within their frames and drew painted weapons. Robes and cloaks snapped in a wind that had no business blowing indoors, the current circling tighter until it gathered itself into the storm-edged shape of a man.

He introduced himself in a voice that filled the hall: [[Emir Suleiman al-Zahir]], custodian of the Elminster Annex. Then he asked why a band of armed strangers had Teleported directly into the middle of his collection.

Now that no one was staring exclusively at the Mandolin, the character of the hall became obvious. Its cases preserved fragments of Elminster's long wake through history: a depleted wand once carried by an apprentice and a lord's journal describing the Shadow Princes of [[Shade Enclave]] descending upon the Dales.

But [[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]] had not crossed the world for the journals. He had come for the Mandolin—one of [[The Instruments of the Bards|the seven Instruments of the Bards]]—and said so.

[[Emir Suleiman al-Zahir|Al-Zahir]] was entrusted with the Annex's security. He would permit nothing to leave its case. He would, however, take them to the man empowered to negotiate.

## A Borrowed Shape

On the way to the Dean's office, [[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]] studied the Djinni and decided he understood the situation. Why, he asked, would a being like al-Zahir allow himself to be enslaved here? Cirrus knew what it was to be a Djinn. He had used True Polymorph to become one before.

Al-Zahir did not accept Cirrus's claim to understanding.

Borrowing the body and powers of a Djinn did not give Cirrus its life, its history, or its people. He could not know what it meant to live among the noble houses of Ossa merely because a spell had once given him their shape. The Djinni called the claim cultural appropriation with enough finality to end the conversation. He never confirmed that his service to Candlekeep was slavery at all.

Cirrus agreed to disagree.

They stopped before an ornate office door emblazoned with the golden sigil of [[Mystra]]. Al-Zahir snapped his fingers. The door opened.

## Dean Rochester

[[Dean Rochester]] was a thin, elderly human with a monk's tonsure and brass spectacles. His robes were so elaborately worked in shining gold that they made ordinary academic finery look funereal, and precious rings crowded his fingers. He studied the intruders, seemed to take the measure of their power, and smiled knowingly before the bargaining began.

Then JmJm pushed to the front and introduced himself, and the smile tightened.

Rochester looked past him to Fëanon. Candlekeep, he remarked, knew of *Fëanon of the Lady's College*. The contrast seemed calculated. One visitor had arrived demanding a treasure; the other came with the weight of an established scholarly reputation.

JmJm explained that he needed the Mandolin.

The Dean was not wholly opposed. Its donor had left an explicit condition: the Instrument could pass only to a bardic hero proven sufficiently worthy. Rochester presented that condition as a duty rather than a price. What he could do was judge the claimant.

There was, conveniently, a test.

## A Century Overdue

A particular book had been borrowed from Candlekeep a century earlier and never returned. The college had finally traced the volume to [[Acheron]], the Plane of Endless War: an afterlife of goblinoid souls, marching [[Modrons]], the distant machinery of the Blood War, and great metal cubes tumbling through an endless grey void.

Recover the book, Rochester said, and Candlekeep could determine what sort of hero JmJm was.

JmJm scoffed. He did not have time to submit himself to an elderly librarian's opinion. He stood, declared the entire proposition ridiculous, and left the office still complaining.

[[Aliyah Faen Tlabbar|Patches]] tried the other door through the problem. She contacted [[Lady Faye]] and asked how much influence could be bought inside Candlekeep. The answer was useful even if it did not solve the immediate difficulty: the [[The Zhentarim|Zhentarim]] had little reach within the college. Lady Faye could lend her ten thousand gold with which to grease palms, but she could not promise that any of those palms would matter.

Back in the office, Rochester found a second grievance. Their Teleportation had *broken* the Annex wards, he claimed, and repairs would require payment. Fëanon suspected the Dean had simply become obstinate after JmJm's performance, but there was more value in mollifying him than in winning the point.

Terms emerged. Candlekeep would lend them a planar tuning fork keyed to Acheron, on the condition that they obtain a replacement on the plane before returning. The High Elf borrower had left an Ioun stone as collateral; Fëanon could use it as a sympathetic link to find him. [[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]] separately negotiated for a fork attuned to [[Abyss|the Abyss]]. And while the others worked through the expedition, Fëanon and Rochester found their own common ground: an exchange of rare and extra-planar books between Candlekeep and Fëanon's collection, mutually beneficial and entirely independent of the bard glowering outside.

Rochester gave them a cottage for the night in Candlekeep's visitors' town, among shops selling scholarly supplies and lodgings built for travellers who arrived carrying more questions than answers.

## Twenty-Five Thousand Reasons

The evening was quiet. JmJm's complaints were not.

He grumbled about the Dean until Patches suggested that Rochester might simply be lonely and curmudgeonly. JmJm went still. Somewhere behind the annoyance, an idea found its stage.

He cast Wish.

Twenty-five thousand gold pieces appeared for Dean Rochester, accompanied by a personalised note.

The following morning an Instant Messenger arrived in the shape of a tiny, lean Rochester and delivered the Dean's thanks. JmJm watched it depart and laughed. Some people, he decided, were very easy to manipulate.

The Dean, notably, had not waived the trial.

Al-Zahir came to the cottage in a form that looked much more human than the storm-bodied custodian they had first met. He presented the party with a rusted iron planar fork. Cirrus accepted it eagerly, and the group prepared for a plane whose entire geography was trying to kill something.

They joined hands. The cottage vanished.

## The Plane of Endless War

They arrived inside a dim, smoky hall built from scrap metal.

Goblins, Orcs, and Hobgoblins crowded rough tables beneath a haze of cookfire and noise. Flagons were raised. Meat turned over open flame. Every face in the room swung toward the strangers who had appeared without warning in the middle of the feast.

The silence held for one breath.

Then the hall erupted. Dozens of goblinoids seized weapons and charged.

Fëanon answered with *Fëanon's Deep Slumber*. Most of the first wave folded where they stood, collapsing across benches and one another as the spell rolled through them. The group ignored the lesser combatants and fixed on the largest Orc in the hall—a brute who had remained seated, watching to see how the newcomers handled themselves.

The party killed him and his entourage in moments.

The attack stopped. The hall looked from the fallen boss to the people who had killed him, reconsidered the local hierarchy, and began to cheer. Flagons rose again. Roasted joints of meat were thrust toward the victors, their provenance left tactfully unexamined.

JmJm leapt onto a table.

If they were going to cross a plane of endless war, he proclaimed, then they required a war party. These Goblins and Orcs would do nicely. He gave them a High Elf wizard to hunt, a lost book to seize, and the promise of every fight between here and there. By the time he finished, the hall was howling for conquest.

The army rushed outside.

## The Great Cube

Acheron did not have a horizon so much as an argument between directions.

The hall stood on one face of an immense metal cube. Beyond its edge stretched an endless grey void crowded with other cubes—some no larger than dice, others the size of planetoids—turning slowly past one another. War covered every visible surface. A cube laden with hundreds of Goblins skimmed past their own, close enough for its inhabitants to leap the gap. Many landed. Many missed and fell screaming into the grey.

Fëanon took the borrower's Ioun stone and scried.

The vision found a spelljammer broken across the metal landscape, its timbers and hull plating rusted and rotted by decades of neglect. Skeletal undead and wraiths moved through the wreckage. A cloaked shape flitted between the ruined compartments, less a living wizard than an idea that had died badly. Fëanon recognised an [[Allips|Allip]] and suspected it was what remained of the High Elf who had borrowed the book.

The pattern of cubes in the vision matched their sky. Fëanon judged that the wreck was probably on this same Great Cube, only a few faces away—but reaching it meant crossing dozens of miles of territory whose inhabitants fought because fighting was the only stable fact their afterlife possessed.

The party chose to speak in the plane's native language.

Their war party would roll across the cube, crush whatever opposed it, and absorb the survivors. JmJm rode on a sled of scrap pulled by an Ogre, playing to the marching host. Patches climbed into a makeshift crow's nest above him and watched the scarred metal ahead. Their following gathered strength as it travelled.

## The Injured Modron

In a deeply gouged stretch of the cube, cries rose from one edge of the column. Several Goblins had found an injured [[Modrons|Modron]]. Cornered and damaged, the construct defended itself efficiently enough to leave bodies around it. The rest of the war party surged forward to tear it apart.

[[Gundren Rockseeker|Gundren]] reached it first and stopped them.

He repaired the Modron where it stood, brass and mechanism answering hands that had spent a lifetime learning machinery—and a century unknowingly carrying a fragment of Modron order within his own mind. Once functional, the creature told him of a Modron channel or passage several miles ahead. It lay in the same direction as the wreck.

They took the construct with them.

The edge of the cube face drew near, bringing the disorienting problem of crossing from one field of gravity to another. Before they reached it, another war party crested the scarred metal horizon. It was larger than their own.

The two hosts accelerated and collided in a brutal roar of iron, teeth, and dead souls.

The party drove straight for the rival leaders. If they killed or subjugated the warbosses quickly enough, they could inherit the army before both sides tore each other to pieces.

On Acheron, that counted as diplomacy.
