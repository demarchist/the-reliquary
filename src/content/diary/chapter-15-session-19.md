## The Warbosses

The party hit the rival command before the two war parties could grind one another away.

The enemy warbosses were strong enough to have ruled hundreds of dead soldiers on [[Acheron]]. They were not strong enough to survive the people who had come for them. One by one they fell, their bodies dissolving as the great machinery of the plane reclaimed their souls for another turn through its endless wars.

The surviving goblinoids saw their leaders vanish and joined the victors. The party's following swelled into the hundreds.

Then a shadow began to descend from the void.

At first it was another of Acheron's tumbling cubes, vast and dark against the grey. As it drew closer, something moved upon its face—a black, oily mass that could not hold one shape for more than a heartbeat. Hundreds of arms reached from it. Legs, claws, torsos, warriors, and beasts surfaced and sank through one another, a thousand bodies kneaded into a single shifting form.

Goblinoids on both sides dropped to their knees.

*Warform.*

## The Demon of War

The great [[Warform]] rode its cube closer while the last warboss died. The party rallied its newly combined host and drove it toward the edge of the Great Cube, but an army of hundreds could not turn quickly enough.

The Warform leapt.

It struck the cube with the force of a small earthquake and landed among the stragglers. Hundreds of weapon-bearing arms rose and fell. Claws tore through bodies. Gouts of acid washed across packed ranks. The thing made no speech and offered no challenge; it simply converted whatever it reached into more evidence of what Acheron was.

The party looked back and kept moving. The Goblins were already dead, already bound to a plane that would recycle them into battle. The group had come for a book, not to measure itself against every horror that called endless war home.

The survivors followed them. So did the Warform.

## Over the Edge

Gravity belonged separately to every face of the cube. Crossing an edge meant persuading the body that the wall ahead was about to become the ground below.

The party jumped, flew, and scrambled through the transition while the war party piled after them. [[Tarkathee Daru|Tark]] lost his orientation and began to fall into the sky. The new [[Windrunner Armor|Windrunner robes]] answered before panic could: gravity became a matter of choice, and the monk floated easily back to the new face. [[Gundren Rockseeker|Gundren]] tumbled from [[Arkto]]'s saddle and caught it before the void could claim him.

[[Fëanon Sërindë|Fëanon]] reached the new plane of gravity, turned, and watched the mass of war-torn souls bearing down on the edge behind them.

He had an idea.

At the instant the Warform began to cross between faces, Fëanon cast Wish and laid a Reverse Gravity effect across it. The fiend did not roar. It did not bargain. It simply fell upward, a thousand silent limbs reaching through the grey until the void swallowed it from sight.

The surviving war party made the crossing with only a handful of souls lost. Miles ahead, the broken spelljammer lay black against the metal plain.

## The Lunar Wreck

The army surged across the new face and crushed whatever tried to bar its way. At the wreck, the war party and its leaders cleared the Swordwraiths and lingering dead from the ruined vessel.

The Allip moved through the corridors like a ragged absence. If it had once been the High Elf who borrowed Candlekeep's book, nothing recognisably elven remained in it. The group destroyed the spirit and found the missing volume in the remains of the captain's quarters.

The mission was complete. [[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]] kept looking.

The spelljammer had been stripped over the decades. No meaningful artifice or magic remained, nothing to justify the trouble of hauling a ruined hull across worlds. Yet its artistry felt familiar to him. The lines, ornament, and workmanship suggested a vessel of the Lunar Elves, and he wanted it.

The absence of practical value did not make the [[Lunar Spelljammer|wreck]] worthless. It made it a stage waiting for someone with imagination.

## An Army Through the Gate

The party opened a Gate to the [[Thunder Bluff|Barony]]. The war party became a salvage crew.

Goblinoid souls heaved pieces of timber and metal through the portal while Tark altered the gravity around the largest sections and let them fall from one plane into the other. The first carriers managed only a few steps into the living world before losing corporeality. Their exact disposition beyond the threshold was unclear; the wreckage they carried still crashed and clattered across the ground outside the Gate, while the war party remained behind on Acheron.

Barony guards arrived expecting an invasion and found a portal vomiting ship parts and briefly corporeal Goblins instead. [[Bohd]] swept in on his wyvern, spotted the party through the opening, and called for an explanation.

The explanation was necessarily brief.

Bohd ordered the guards to drag the debris clear so the flow could continue. The war party passed most of the vessel through in many, many pieces. With the missing book secure and the last useful wreckage salvaged, the group crossed home before the Gate expired.

They left their army behind. On Acheron, there would always be another war.

## The Bard

Exhaustion brought them to [[The Establishment]] and an evening meal.

From there they sent [[Candlekeep|Candlekeep's]] book and a planar fork back by Instant Messenger. The return delivery contained the promised [[The Instruments of the Bards|Mandolin]], the Abyssal planar fork [[Cirrus Al Kayan|Cirrus]] had negotiated for, and a note addressed simply to *The Bard*.

[[Jack Mayer Johnson Mathews|JmJm]] had refused to let [[Dean Rochester]] judge his worth. He had then crossed the Plane of Endless War, raised an army with a speech, recovered a century-lost book, and delivered it anyway.

Whatever Candlekeep had decided about the method, the Mandolin was his.

The companions separated and returned to their homes.

## The Teeth

Cirrus reached the [[Fane of Selune|Fane]] to find [[Topsy & Turvy|Topsy]] waiting for him, visibly nervous.

There had been a delivery.

She led him into the main hall. The great statue of Cirrus still towered over the sanctuary, but its feet had disappeared beneath thousands upon thousands of bloody teeth. They lay in drifts across the floor, an obscene parody of an offering left inside the home he had built for [[Selune]].

Topsy handed him the accompanying letter.

It was from [[Zulkhir Atlan Issan|Zulkhir Atlan]]: Thayan Archmage, [[The Warlocks of Ceregorach|Warlock of Ceregorach]], and the enemy who had escaped the Aimu Estate with black blood on the grass behind him. Atlan goaded Cirrus to return to the [[Bloodstone Lands]] and face him, naming himself one of only two servants of the [[Ceregorach|Tooth Fairy]] who remained. The teeth placed the material language of Cirrus's broken pact at the feet of the life he had built beneath Selûne; whether Atlan understood every layer of that threat or simply knew it would wound him, the provocation was intimate.

Cirrus looked from the letter to the teeth.

"Pack them up and send them to my chambers," he told Topsy. "Do not disturb me after. I will be building this night."

The doors closed behind him.
